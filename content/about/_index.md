Autumn and the Pinholes started in a village hall during a particularly wet October, when Autumn had a shoebox of half-finished songs, Sam had a fiddle that needed re-strung, and nobody had a gig booked.

We play modern folk. That means old-fashioned instruments (fiddle, guitar, a melodica that Autumn refuses to put down) playing songs about now: allotments, bad weather, busted bicycles, the moon, and the odd unfortunate badger.

The name comes from two things. "Autumn" is the singer, and "the Pinholes" is everybody else, which is Sam and the cat. It is also what the light looks like when it comes through a tiny hole in a shed wall. We think that is a good way to make a record.

We are happiest in small rooms with wonky floorboards, and we will play in your kitchen if you ask nicely and promise not to mind the cat.
